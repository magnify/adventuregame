# The Door in the Tree

As told to his daughter. Written down as told, nothing added.
Where the game needs something the story doesn't yet say, it's marked **[open]**.

## The door

A girl walks down the street and sees something in a tree. She climbs up and
looks closer and closer. A small door, with a keyhole. Locked. She thinks about
how to open it, and of course: look under the doormat. The key is there and fits
like a glove. She turns it and opens the door.

A bright light shines out and blinds her for a moment. When her eyes adjust she
sees a meadow. She leans closer and is pulled through.

The door slams shut behind her. The key rattles out of the lock and lands neatly
under the doormat, which falls back into place and covers it.

## The world with three suns

A strange world. Three suns in the sky. Insects everywhere, and they talk.

One of them is **Barlin** *(placeholder name, easy to change)*, a butterfly in a
top hat. He leads the girl into the world.

## The forest

Barlin takes her into a forest. It gets darker the further they go, slowly.

They come to a fork in the road. They take the narrow path.

After a while it is mysteriously dark. Will-o'-the-wisps appear and lead them
on, into a swamp.

## The swamp

The girl gets stuck in the swamp mud.

Barlin knows a little magic. He makes a stick larger, and it floats over to her
so she can pull herself out.

The stick is her first weapon.

## The swamp witch

In the swamp they see the witch's house. The witch beckons them inside. She is
overly friendly.

Barlin warns the girl: don't eat anything. Drink is fine.

The witch knows Barlin's name without being told. And she says out loud all the
things they talk about.

**This is where the story is now.**

## Notes for the game

Things the story already gives the game, without inventing anything:

- **The rule.** "Don't eat, drink is fine" is a rule the player has to keep while
  the witch keeps offering. That's a scene on its own.
- **The witch hears everything.** If she says aloud whatever they say, they can't
  plan out loud in front of her. In a game with no words, that fits: they have to
  understand each other in pictures. **[open]** what they need to plan, and why.
- **The fork.** The narrow path is the one they take. **[open]** whether the wide
  path is ever walked, or exists only to be not chosen.
- **The wisps.** They lead. **[open]** whether they help or trick.
- **Three suns.** The light in this world is different from home. That's a look,
  and it's free.
- **The stick.** She keeps it. It's the first thing she carries that isn't a key,
  and "first weapon" says there will be a second, and something to use them on.
  **[open]** what.
- **Barlin's magic.** He can make small things bigger. Once the player has seen
  it, they'll want to use it. **[open]** whether it's his alone, or something
  she can ask for.

Things the game will need that only the storyteller can give:

- **[open]** What the witch wants.
- **[open]** How the girl gets out, and whether Barlin comes with her.
- **[open]** What "helping" this world means, if this is the world she helps.
